Quinten sucessfully defended his MSc Thesis titled `The influence of  road
surface on  power loss: The design of a experimental apparatus that mimics road
surfaces and measures power loss
<https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:b43d8df3-a89a-4c0e-8f67-8cdc8623f89c>`_
on July 16, 2026.

Quinten designed and developed a variable road surface treadmill with 3D
printed paver bricks capable of accurately measuring the energy loss due to
rolling with or without a rider. He used a custom flexure based load cell
system to get very accurate measurements so that we can differentiate among
small variations in tires, posture, or suspension. The following video shows
the treadmill in action.

Quinten measured the rolling losses of a bicycle with rider over paver bricks
for speeds up to 30 km/h and a range of tire pressures. The following plot
shows the speed and tire pressure relationship along with the low uncertainty
in the measurements. It is remarkable how much losses are due to vibration. At
30 km/h 150 Watts of about 200 Watts are due to vibrations of the system and 50
due to traditional rolling resistance measures. This new measurement system
will help us further crack open the research on vibrational power loss.
