Dana successfully defended her MSc Thesis titled `Effects of Assistance Level
and Familiarization on Elderly Riding a Balance Assist Bicycle
<https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:764633b0-c1e0-420a-b057-ecfb94c77180>`_
on July 15, 2026.

Dana investigated whether elderly riders felt comfortable riding our balance
assist bicycle and whether the assistance had any effect on motion. She did
this by having three groups of elderly riders test the bicycle that were
unaware of the assistance strength level: off, low, or high. The subjects were
only told that the bicycle had something akin to power steering in a car, but
not that it had a stabilizing effect. Secondly, she tested if the comfort
changed the longer the subjects road the bicycle by having them get familiar
with the bicycle over a long period of riding in one day. Interestingly, she
did not confirm the findings we had in [Alizadehsaravi2023]_, i.e. that the
balance assist reduced steering and rolling motions. She also found that the 31
elderly subjects' comfort opinion over time improved for all levels of
assistance but it was not apparent that subjects gave differing comfort
opinions for different assistance levels, possibly pointing towards the riders'
inability to detect the assistance. This study has given us another set of
clues to understand if balance assist really assists and whether riders will
accept it. Elderly riders will likely accept the system but our confidence it
objectively helps has not grown.
