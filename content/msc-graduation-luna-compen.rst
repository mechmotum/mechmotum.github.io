Luna successfully defended her thesis `Vestibular contributions to cycling
balance across different speeds
<https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:bb8a52f9-02c8-4b55-902a-6f2bc5a65a9b>`_
on September 11, 2026.

Luna measured the kinematics and muscle activity of cyclists balancing on a
treadmill while being perturbed with electrical vestibular stimulation (EVS).
The subjects road at different speeds to vary the difficulty in the balance
task. She extracted the relevant bicycle kinematics (steer rate and roll rate)
from optimal motion capture data. The vestibular stimulation increased steering
motion and muscle activation indicating the connection from vestibular sensing
to balance. EVS correlated with muscle activation in all measured arm muscles
but not the lower back muscle, indicating that vestibular sensing is primary
translated to our arm control actuation. The EVS stimulation detected in the
steering motion decreased with cycling speed as the vehicle became more
self-stable.

.. figure:: https://mechmotum.s3.us-east-005.dream.io/luna-plot.png
   :width: 80%
   :align: center

   Figures showing the detection of the EVS in several muscles and a speed
   dependent effect (in the deltoid).

This may both be the first comprehensive investigation of the role upper body
muscles play in steering and balancing a bicycle as well as the first use of
EVS to start to begin to unravel how we make use of our vestibular organ in
cycling.

Luna was supervised by Patrick Forbes, Matto Leeuwis, and Jason K. Moore.
Everyone at the bicycle lab is very proud of Luna and wishes her the best with
her next endeavors.
