Looka successfully defended his MSc Thesis titled `Freewheeling in Virtual
Space: Design of a Single-Track Vehicle Simulator for Simulating Sustained
Turning Motions
<https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:dd87b218-debb-4c62-95a5-5e057d3711db>`_
on May 1, 2026.

Looka invented, designed, built, and tested an innovative single-track vehicle
simulator. We believe it is the first such simulator to have both natural
balancing through steering and continuous rotation for turning. Coupled with
virtual speed-dependent stability, this will offer the most realistic
reproduction of what it feels like to ride a bicycle while stationary within a
laboratory setting. Looka's process started with a multibody dynamic model of
the system coupled to both a simulator and human controller to prove the system
would behave like a single-track vehicle. See one of his simulations below:

He then designed and constructed the simulator to prove that it works. See the
video below for a demonstration of Looka riding the simulator after his
graduation. The simulator is balanced via steering, includes natural
countersteering, and in this mode is similar to balancing a bicycle at very low
speeds, i.e. no self-stability active.

Furthermore, Looka went beyond his thesis and worked with us and TU Delft's
innovation team to submit a patent application on this concept as well as
co-wrote an NWO Demonstrator grant proposal for the project. This was an
extraordinary effort from an MSc student. Looka was supervised by Jason K. Moore
and José Alfonso Farías Fuentes. Everyone at the bicycle lab is very proud of
Looka and wishes him the best with his next endeavors. We are also excited to
start doing research with his simulator!
