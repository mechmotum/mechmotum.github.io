Philipp successfully defended his MSc Thesis titled `Effect of Tyre
Characteristics on Bicycle Recovery Following Kickplate Perturbations
<https://urn.kb.se/resolve?urn=urn:nbn:se:kth:diva-391166>`_ in July of 2026.
He was a KTH Stockholm MSc student, but did his thesis project work at TU Delft
in collaboration with the two universities.

Philipp made use of our kickplate to study the overall bicycle motion response
with different tire pressures while the rider's upper torso was restrained or
free to move normally. He made use of our "Davis Instrumented Bicycle" for the
restraint and for its ability to measure steer torque accurately in addition to
the vehicle's kinematics.

.. figure:: https://mechmotum.s3.us-east-005.dream.io/jonas-kick.png
   :align: center

   Bicycle while being kicked by the kickplate.

He found that tire pressure did change recovery response but there was not a
monotonic change with respect to tire pressure. With regards to the upper torso
restraint, he found larger differences between restrained and not than seen in
differing tire pressures. This points towards a dominant effect of
rider-bicycle relative motion to the overall response.

Philipp was supervised by Gabriele Dell'Orto, Riender Happee, and Jason K.
Moore with Malte Rothhämel as his KTH examiner. Everyone at the bicycle lab
wishes Philipp the best in his future endeavors; which will surely include his
passion for sailing!
