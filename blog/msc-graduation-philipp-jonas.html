Philipp Jonas Successfully Defends His MSc Thesis

  • Sat 21 November 2026
  • news
  • Jason K. Moore
  • #tire, #slip, #kickplate, #experiment

Philipp successfully defended his MSc Thesis titled Effect of Tyre Characteristics on Bicycle Recovery Following Kickplate Perturbations in July of 2026. He was a KTH Stockholm MSc student, but did his thesis project work at TU Delft in collaboration with the two universities.

Philipp made use of our kickplate to study the overall bicycle motion response with different tire pressures while the rider's upper torso was restrained or free to move normally. He made use of our "Davis Instrumented Bicycle" for the restraint and for its ability to measure steer torque accurately in addition to the vehicle's kinematics.

Bicycle while being kicked by the kickplate.

He found that tire pressure did change recovery response but there was not a monotonic change with respect to tire pressure. With regards to the upper torso restraint, he found larger differences between restrained and not than seen in differing tire pressures. This points towards a dominant effect of rider-bicycle relative motion to the overall response.

Philipp was supervised by Gabriele Dell'Orto, Riender Happee, and Jason K. Moore with Malte Rothhämel as his KTH examiner. Everyone at the bicycle lab wishes Philipp the best in his future endeavors; which will surely include his passion for sailing!
